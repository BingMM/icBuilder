# Session — Reflattening coregistration correction

Last reviewed: 2026-08-27

## Complete-orbit requirement

The native debugging IDLs are partial extracts, not complete orbits: 20 frames
per sensor for orbits 0085/0086 and three for 0968. Consequently, any
diagnostic that estimates one constant over the loaded stack or refits the
time-dependent fuvpy background is exploratory. This qualification applies to
the reflat, histogram-background, recovered-calibration, and SI-profile-
smoothing results. Complete-corpus Product-2 statistics, source/table tracing,
and synthetic mapping checks remain valid. No partial stack may be used to
accept or reject a background or calibration explanation.

Deferred test: obtain all native WIC/SI12/SI13 IDLs from one complete orbit;
estimate the constant independently per frame using off-Earth and deep-
nightside masks, median/mode `I/F`, and robust `I`-versus-`F` regression; test
on held-out rows, columns, or frames; bootstrap at frame/region level; require
the corrected background to be independent of detector row and flat-field
factor; and only then propagate the inferred interval through the ratio
pipeline. DMSP is validation data and must not be used to choose the constant.

The recovered-FUVVIEW3 reflattening diagnostic incorrectly used direct
Delaunay centre-value interpolation for SI12/SI13-to-WIC mapping. It now uses
the established Coumans-style detector-footprint method: SI pixels are
represented as quadrilaterals, distributed according to overlap with native
WIC detector pixels, and accepted only when area coverage is at least 90%.

The method was extracted into `icbuilder/detector_coregistration.py` and is
shared by the Coumans and reflattening diagnostics. An orbit-0968 frame-2
comparison gives exact agreement with the original Coumans mapping, including
the sparse overlap matrix, mapped values, coverage, and geometry diagnostics.
Two synthetic tests verify constant-field preservation and coverage rejection.

All reflattening products were regenerated for orbits 0085, 0086, and 0968.
Relative to the former point interpolation, the corrected mapping lowers the
current-fuvpy median ratio by 12.50, 13.25, and 0.67 ratio units and lowers the
above-Frey fraction by 5.58, 4.04, and 0.58 percentage points. This is a
material mapping effect for 0085 and 0086.

The calibration conclusion is nevertheless unchanged. Adding recovered SI13
to recovered WIC raises the ratio's 90th percentile and above-Frey fraction in
all three examples. The above-Frey increases are 2.78, 1.18, and 0.62
percentage points. Applying recovered SI12 afterward changes the result
negligibly. The tested SI reflattening does not repair the large-ratio problem.

The regenerated directory includes `coregistration_summary.csv` alongside the
ratio summaries and maps. The shared helper was added for diagnostics, but the
production Product-1/Product-2 pipeline was not changed.

## Orbit-0968 frame and SI flat-field check

Explicit ratio maps were generated for all three available orbit-0968 frames.
The detector-horizontal ratio band occurs in frames 0, 1, and 2. Median row
profiles contain repeated jumps around WIC rows 49--56, so the feature is not a
single-frame plotting artifact.

The recovered calibration tables contain large genuine row-to-row changes. In
orbit 0085 the adjacent SI13 factors change by a median 3.1%, a 95th percentile
24.5%, and a maximum 69%; SI12 gives 3.7%, 19.7%, and 36%. WIC gives only
0.4%, 1.1%, and 1.9%. Orbit 0085 occurs on day 137, whereas nearest pre-boom
profiles are day 183 for SI13 and day 255 for WIC/SI12. Weak-signal horizontal
residuals could therefore plausibly be calibration imprints.

The multiplicative SI profile has now been tested directly. The diagnostic
divides out the selected recovered SI12/SI13 factor, reapplies a
median-preserving Gaussian-smoothed factor (`sigma=2` or `5` detector rows),
and refits the background before coregistration and proton correction. Relative
to current fuvpy, smoothing increases the fixed nightside median ratio by
4.4%/5.2% for orbit 0085 and 2.2%/6.8% for orbit 0086. Orbit 0968 changes by
+3.8%/-0.5%. Above-Frey fractions are unchanged or worse except for a
negligible 0.3 percentage-point reduction in the strongest orbit-0968 case.
Detector-row plots show redistribution rather than systematic removal of the
horizontal structure. Simple smoothing is not a defensible calibration fix,
and production remains unchanged. The example SI files use calibration flag 1
and zero `AIRGLOW_SCALE`, so multiplying positive saved pixels by
`F_smooth/F_original` exactly replaces the multiplicative factor in the
recovered FUVVIEW3 equation. Values already clipped to zero upstream are not
recoverable and do not enter the fixed positive support.

The post-boom SI13 profile selected for orbit 0968 is visibly less spiky than
the pre-boom orbit-0085 profile: their 95th-percentile adjacent-row changes are
10.8% and 24.5%, respectively. The same smoothing widths were retained across
the boom boundary so the experiment reflects that difference naturally.

## Additive constant versus overall sensor gain

The constant in Ohma et al.'s WIC reflat is not a stored FUVVIEW3 calibration
number. FUVVIEW3's explicit `bkg_level` is zero for the example SI files
because `AIRGLOW_SCALE=0`. fuvpy instead estimates the physical constant
background `C` from the median of unflattened WIC pixels with `SZA>100`, using
one scalar for the complete loaded stack and falling back to 450 counts. For
the orbit-sized examples it estimates about 411, 417, and 1032 counts for
orbits 0085, 0086, and 0968. Diagnostic SI13 estimates are about 1.88, 1.88,
and 5.04 counts. These values test the reflat assumption; they are not an
independent determination of the true detector background.

Per-frame estimates expose the limitation. Orbits 0085/0086 contain 20 frames
but only about 220--320 candidate SI dark pixels per frame; 10--24% of SI13
candidates are clipped zeros. Orbit 0968 contains only three frames but about
6,500--8,200 candidates per frame. Its SI13 estimates are 4.08, 5.01, and 6.42
counts, and SI12 gives 5.51, 5.98, and 6.89. The issue is therefore systematic
contamination and clipping, not simply the number of pooled pixels.

FUVVIEW3 separately applies a daily multiplicative mission correction derived
from stellar sensitivity, interpolated over missing days, median-filtered over
five days, and normalized independently for each camera to 2000 day 171. In
the recovered table, the WIC/SI13 correction ratio is 0.703 for orbit 0085,
0.802 for orbit 0086, and 1.456 for orbit 0968. The correction therefore lowers
the first two ratios but raises the Coumans-event ratio by about 46%. The exact
table used by the dataset producer and its compatibility with the Frey response
calibration remain unverified. The smoothing experiment preserved each
profile's median and therefore did not test this scalar gain.

## Flat-field time history and provenance

`plot_fuview3_flatfield_history.py` now plots every stored profile and marks the
profiles selected for orbits 0085 and 0968. FUVVIEW3 chooses the nearest profile
independently for each sensor but only on the same side of the boom loss. It
does not interpolate in time; an exact tie selects the first, chronologically
earlier database entry. Orbit 0085 has no preceding pre-boom profile: its
selected SI13 profile is 46 days later and its WIC/SI12 profiles are 118 days
later.

Consecutive same-boom-state WIC profiles differ by about 0.9% in the median row
and less than 6.3% at the largest row. SI differences are much larger. The two
post-boom SI12 profiles differ by 12.6% in the median and 72% at the 95th
percentile, with extreme edge outliers. Several consecutive SI13 pairs differ
by 6--11% in the median and 30--41% at the 95th percentile. The figure and full
table are saved as `flatfield_profiles_over_time.png` and
`flatfield_profile_differences.csv`.

Frey et al. (2003) document that the operational factors were derived from
low-altitude, sunlit middle-latitude dayglow images. The observed dayglow was
normalized for viewing geometry with a dayglow model and then to the image
centre. WIC deviations were fitted smoothly, while SI deviations were retained
directly in lookup tables. This explains why SI can preserve noisy row-scale
structure. Coumans et al. (2004) describe instrument-specific quiet-time
airglow subtraction but no custom or event-specific flat field. Standard team
calibration, possibly from a different FUVVIEW3 revision, is more likely than a
custom Coumans profile.

A defensible independent flat-field reconstruction would require unflatfielded
detector data, many quiet low-altitude dayglow images, geometry and solar-flux
normalization, robust auroral/limb masking, and held-out validation. The current
IDL `IMAGE` arrays are already flatfielded, so deriving new factors from them
would be circular. Smoothing and reapplying the existing SI profiles is an easy
sensitivity test, not a new calibration.

## WIC calibration flag provenance

A full text search of the supplied FUVVIEW3 tree found image-calibration flags
0 (raw), 1 (corrected counts), and 2 (Rayleigh) in the GUI and calculation
path. The only other literal `flag=3` is the output-format option of an
unrelated generic `datatype.pro` utility. The local WIC IDLs nevertheless
contain `CALIBRATION_FLAG=3` and `CIMAGE`, while neither appears in the
supplied image structures or calibration code. The supplied tree is therefore
not the exact WIC producer version, or a separate export step modified those
records. This blocks a definitive interpretation of WIC flag 3 from the
available source alone.

All 43 available WIC examples contain flag 3 and a 256-by-256 `CIMAGE`; none of
the 43 SI12 or 43 SI13 examples contains `CIMAGE`, and their flags are all 1.
`CIMAGE` can be negative, `IMAGE-CIMAGE` is everywhere non-negative, and the
difference affects 30--85% of each WIC frame with maxima near 13,390 counts.
It resembles an unknown background-subtracted WIC product but is not identical
to current fuvpy `dgimg` or `shimg`.

Fixed terminology: the stellar record determines the multiplicative mission
correction `B_s(t)` relative to 2000 day 171, and `B_WIC/B_SI13` scales the
image ratio. The Ohma reflat uses the separate additive detector-background
constant `C_s`, estimated by fuvpy from the loaded WIC images. The planned
complete-orbit test estimates `C`, not `B`.

The native detector-grid NetCDFs are sufficient for the complete-orbit `C`
test. SI12/SI13 `img` retains IDL `IMAGE`. Although WIC `img` has already been
reflattened using `C_0`, the dark-pixel relation
`J=C_0+F(C_true-C_0)` allows robust regression of reflattened WIC against the
known flat field: intercept `a=C_0`, slope `b=C_true-C_0`, and
`C_true=a+b`. Original IDLs are therefore unnecessary for this test. Binned
NetCDFs remain unsuitable because they lose detector-row structure.

The scientific value of that test is now narrowed to detector-row artifacts.
WIC has already been reflattened, while `F > 1` across all of SI12 and much of
SI13. A positive SI13 constant would therefore lower much of the denominator
and tend to increase, not repair, WIC/SI13. The remaining global-scale issue is
an unresolved relative WIC/SI13 calibration or incompatibility between the
produced counts and the Frey response calibration. The stellar/mission factor
`B_WIC/B_SI13` has the needed multiplicative leverage, but is not proven to be
incorrect.

The literature review identified a direct final calibration benchmark. Frey et
al. (2003), Figure 16, gives simultaneous numerically scaled SI12, WIC, and
SI13 maps for 28 October 2000 at 11:38 UT, which lies in orbit 0364. Meurant et
al. (2003), Figures 7 and 9, give WIC-count and corrected-ratio keograms for the
same event from 10:04--13:00 UT. The current static Frey-table implementation
also omits the activity-dependent atmospheric correction that Frey explicitly
used when disturbed composition made SI13 too weak and inferred energy
unreasonably high. Reconstruct these published count benchmarks before closing
the ratio investigation.

The first implementation mistakenly reread native IDLs. This was unnecessary:
the sensor-level NetCDFs retain the native detector images, fuvpy corrections,
and geometry. `scripts/debugging/recreate_frey_figure16.py` now reads
`wic_or0364.nc`, `s12_or0364.nc`, and `s13_or0364.nc` directly, without using
binned products or refitting the background. It area-maps SI12/SI13 onto the
native WIC detector, performs proton correction and ratio/energy retrieval, and
writes the six-panel Figure-16 layout. The actual event run selected simultaneous
11:38:24 frames and completed successfully.

## CIMAGE ratio sensitivity

`scripts/debugging/compare_cimage.py` compares current fuvpy WIC with saved
WIC `CIMAGE` while holding SI12, SI13, the 2-keV proton correction, detector-
footprint mapping, and comparison support fixed. On nightside support, median
ratios change current -> CIMAGE by 199.7 -> 216.4 for orbit 0085, 146.3 ->
164.1 for 0086, and 92.0 -> 99.1 for 0968. Fractions above the Frey maximum
change 0.742 -> 0.809, 0.529 -> 0.592, and 0.168 -> 0.202. `CIMAGE` removes a
strong smooth background/dayglow component from the saved `IMAGE`, but it does
not repair the high ratio in these partial-orbit examples. Production was not
changed.
