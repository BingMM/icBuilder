# Session — IMAGE ratio failure trace

Last reviewed: 2026-08-13

## Result

Added and ran `scripts/debugging/debug_image_ratio.py` on example orbits 0085
and 0086. The script is read-only with respect to scientific products and
writes diagnostics under `figures/debugging/`.

The first confirmed failure boundary is the common-grid WIC/SI13 ratio before
proton correction, not the response-table inversion:

| Orbit | Median strong ratio before | Inside table before | Median after | Inside table after |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0085 | 208.2 | 14.6% | 242.9 | 8.9% |
| 0086 | 177.5 | 30.4% | 193.5 | 26.8% |

The Frey response-table interval is 34.84--136.49. After correction, only 1.6%
and 5.4% of all finite orbit pixels use an interior-table retrieval. Most use
weak-signal fallbacks or high-ratio saturation. Proton correction worsens the
distribution but does not create the problem.

The detector-stage figures confirm that SI13 saved by fuvpy reproduces the IDL
image, while WIC changes through the configured `reflat=True` path. The current
example set cannot establish whether the remaining mismatch comes from
background subtraction, absolute/cross-camera calibration, atmospheric/viewing
response, or incompatibility with the Frey response tables.

## fuvpy background test

The exact fuvpy paper and local implementation were compared. The paper uses
BS regularization `0.01, 0.1, 0.1` and SH regularization `0.0001, 10, 10` for
WIC, SI12, and SI13. The orbit script currently uses BS `1e-3` and SH `1e-4`
for every camera; Product 1 subsequently uses WIC `shimg` but SI `dgimg`.

The two example orbits were reprocessed in scratch space with both parameter
sets. WIC SH with SI12/SI13 DG remains the best tested combination. The
preferred path after proton correction is:

| Orbit | Current median | Publication median | Current inside | Publication inside |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0085 | 242.9 | 244.0 | 8.9% | 8.9% |
| 0086 | 193.5 | 194.2 | 26.8% | 26.6% |

The publication's strong SI SH damping prevents the severe overfit seen in
the saved SI SH products, but SI SH still worsens the ratio relative to SI DG.
The choice not to apply SH correction to SI12 and SI13 is supported. The
accessible fuvpy background-model choices do not explain the ratio failure.

On fixed common pixels, orbit 0086 shows an increasing ratio with WIC DZA;
orbit 0085 is not monotonic and has inadequate low-DZA support. This keeps
viewing geometry as a plausible contributor but does not justify an empirical
LOS correction. The next unresolved boundary is calibrated-count provenance
and compatibility with the Frey/Coumans cross-camera response.

## Published positive control

Gasparini et al. (2024) used WIC, SI12, and SI13 to derive conductance for
2001-01-04 05:20--07:41 UT. Its method uses a common 250-km grid and a
three-image/six-minute average before clipping and ratio retrieval, followed by
a regularized conductance inversion.

The corresponding orbit 0478 is present in both local conductance archives.
For the publication interval, the regenerated counts give a median
strong-pixel WIC/SI13 ratio of 40.8 with 56.9% inside the Frey table. The
historical corrected ratio has median 41.5 with 56.3% inside. This is a valid
positive control and shows that the ratio failure is not universal.

An approximate three-frame mean of the already binned May examples did not
repair their ratios. The next useful test is therefore a stage-by-stage modular
reproduction of orbit 0478 if its Product-1 camera files can be recovered. The
paper's Zenodo archive contains only WIC and cannot supply the missing SI
channels.

## DZA literature review

The user's cancellation argument is correct: applying the same `cos(DZA)`
factor to WIC and SI13 leaves WIC/SI13 unchanged. The reviewed literature does
not support that operation as an energy-ratio correction.

Ohma et al. (2024) gives the only clear numerical guidance found. Its fuvpy
dayglow model uses `cos(SZA) / cos(DZA)` for optically thin emissions and
states that an upper DZA limit is required and gives 70--80 degrees as an
example because the secant term diverges and geolocation becomes unreliable. This is a
near-limb stability limit, not validation of WIC/SI13 below that angle.

Frey et al. (2003) and Meurant et al. (2003) describe nadir/vertical response
calculations and viewing-dependent line-of-sight and absorption treatment.
Frey et al. explicitly reports that disturbed atmospheric oxygen can absorb
SI13 more strongly, elevate WIC/SI13, and imply unreasonably high electron
energy. No reviewed IMAGE paper supplies a validated ratio-retrieval cutoff.
The plausible DZA dependence is therefore differential absorption,
emission-altitude weighting, auroral structure, and instrument response—not a
shared scalar projection.

Code inspection confirms that `make_orbit_nc_files.py` already asks fuvpy for
`DZA < 75` degrees and that modular Product 1 sets `los_correction=False`.
The conductance figure previously displayed unmasked WIC geometry even where
the ratio had no data; its DZA panel is now restricted to valid WIC/SI13
support. The papers and their relevance are indexed in `literature/README.md`.

## Corpus-level DZA sensitivity

The regenerated Halley corpus was mounted read-only and analyzed with
`scripts/debugging/dza_threshold_sensitivity.py`. The complete Zhang--Paxton
sample contains 1,686 precipitation orbits. The image-ratio generation was
still active, so its file list was frozen at 188 complete orbits (0085--0291).

Relative to the existing `DZA < 75` support, `DZA < 70` retains 97.27% of ZP
product pixels, 94.65% of summed energy flux, 96.23% of summed Pedersen, and
96.31% of summed Hall. The provisional image-ratio snapshot retains 97.45% of
product pixels and 94.22% of summed energy flux. Its median orbit WIC/SI13
ratio changes from 89.81 to 92.54 and the table-interior fraction from 49.01%
to 49.38%; the cutoff therefore does not repair the ratio problem.

The 60-degree stress test removes about 13--14% of product pixels and 23--27%
of summed energy flux. High-DZA cells are a small fraction of coverage but
carry above-average flux. The result is post-hoc: it masks binned WIC median
DZA, does not reprocess raw source pixels, and does not jointly impose the SI
camera angles. Any adopted production cutoff must be tested by regenerating
Product 1 at that limit.

The first plots used medians and interquartile ranges and therefore obscured
the high-ratio tail. Direct tail statistics at 75 degrees show that 25.18% of
supported pixels exceed 150, 14.96% exceed 200, and 5.10% exceed 300; the 90th
percentile is 237.84. Every sampled orbit contains values above 150. Even at
30 degrees, 26.86% exceed 150. Tightening DZA therefore does not eliminate the
observed failure.

A direct two-dimensional density plot of all 4,339,186 supported ratio pixels
in the same 188-orbit snapshot confirms that result. The conditional median is
roughly flat from low to moderate DZA and decreases near 75 degrees. The 90th
and 95th percentiles remain around 220--340 throughout the viewing-angle
range, and the fractions above 150, 200, and 300 generally decrease rather
than increase with DZA. There is no visible positive ratio-versus-DZA trend in
this pooled sample. DZA remains spatially and temporally confounded, but large
DZA does not explain the pathological ratio tail.

The next denominator check finds that 69.7% of supported pixels have corrected
SI13 between 3 and 7.5 counts. Pixels with ratios above 300 have median SI13
of 4.44 counts and median WIC of about 1,780 counts. Small SI13 background,
binning, clipping, or interpolation errors can therefore strongly affect the
tail. Low SI13 is not sufficient by itself: among the smaller population with
SI13 between 20 and 30 counts, 20.9% still exceed a ratio of 150. The next
controlled test should match WIC to SI13's effective 18-by-18 resolution and
compare mean and median aggregation before changing the background model.

## 150-km grid experiment

Setting both WIC and SI grids to 150 km produces 54-by-54 grids but strongly
undersamples SI. In regenerated orbit 0085, WIC has finite binned values in
78.0% of cells, SI12 in 25.1%, and SI13 in 30.6%. SI12 has exactly one source
pixel in 43.5% of all cells, while `BinnedImage` requires at least two finite
pixels before assigning a value.

An additional implementation problem amplifies the loss. Product 2 still
passes SI through bilinear regridding when the SI and WIC grids are identical.
The interpolator requires all four surrounding cells to be finite even when
three have zero mathematical weight. SI12 support consequently falls from
25.1% to 3.7% and SI13 from 30.6% to 7.9%; final image-ratio support is only
1.6%. Identical grids should bypass interpolation. The intended matched-
resolution diagnostic is instead to coarsen WIC to the supported 450-km SI
grid, not refine SI to 150 km.

Product 2 now detects identical source and target grids from their shape and
xi, eta, latitude, and longitude cell-centre arrays. It directly copies SI
signal, uncertainty, and weight, preserving NaNs cell-for-cell. On the user's
current 100-km orbit-0085 files, all 65,373 finite SI12 cells and 69,322 finite
SI13 cells survive preparation. Remaining sparsity is genuine detector
sampling at that grid scale: 62.2% of SI12 and 59.9% of SI13 cells contain no
source pixel.
