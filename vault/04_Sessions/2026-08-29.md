# Session — 2026-08-29

## Frey Figure 16 processing gap

The reconstructed WIC morphology in `frey_figure16_or_0364.png` is visually
close to Frey et al., while the ratio-derived energy is substantially higher.
The display alone is not quantitative because the WIC and SI13 panels are
clipped to fixed color ranges, but it shifts attention toward SI13 and the
ratio construction.

The current reconstruction maps SI footprints onto native WIC pixels, performs
no smoothing, and calculates the ratio for every positive corrected WIC/SI13
pair. Frey et al. do not state a smoothing operation or numerical signal
threshold. They say only that the corrected images were remapped onto a
geomagnetic grid; the current script instead scatters native WIC pixels at their
magnetic coordinates. Meurant et al.'s separate method for the same event does
smooth WIC and SI13 before the ratio, but that is evidence for a sensitivity
test rather than proof of Frey's Figure 16 processing. The undocumented
geomagnetic-grid aggregation, smoothing, and any count threshold must remain
separate hypotheses.

The legacy propagated retrieval does have a `50/3 count` low-signal rule: it
does not evaluate the ratio when corrected SI13 is below 3 counts, but assigns a
fallback energy based partly on whether corrected WIC is below 50 counts. The
Figure 16 reconstruction bypasses that routine and therefore bypasses this
safeguard. Its exact literature provenance remains unknown.

## Frey method-reference audit

The complete Frey et al. (2003) text and its relevant cited methods were
reviewed for omitted Figure-16 processing details. No numerical smoothing
kernel, minimum-count threshold, SNR criterion, geomagnetic-grid resolution,
or remapping/aggregation algorithm is given. Figure 10 also contains no such
operation. Frey's documented sequence is instrument-specific flat-field and
spatial dayglow correction, temporal gain correction, proton subtraction,
WIC/SI13 ratio conversion, and flux conversion. Figure 16 only says that the
corrected counts were remapped to a geomagnetic grid.

The phrase "corrected for temporal changes" refers specifically to Section 6:
whole-image multiplicative sensitivity/gain corrections derived from weekly
stellar observations relative to 19 June 2000. It includes temperature,
high-voltage, and long-term sensitivity changes, not time alignment or temporal
smoothing. Frey does not state that WIC or SI13 was smoothed and does not
mention a one-to-two-pixel kernel. Its only averaging statement concerns the
much finer FAST samples used in the validation comparison. The one-to-two-pixel
image-smoothing statement belongs to Meurant et al. (2003), which Frey does not
cite.

A parallel fuvpy-based Figure-16 sensitivity map was generated with the modern
50/3 signal guard. WIC `shimg` and mapped SI12/SI13 `dgimg` reproduce the clean
top-row inputs. The uncorrected energy is retained where uncorrected WIC >= 50
and SI13 >= 3; the final energy and flux use the same thresholds after proton
correction. The output is
`figures/debugging/paper_reconstruction/frey_figure16_or_0364_mask50_3.{png,pdf}`.

The published uncorrected-to-final change was traced to proton-energy
sensitivity. With SI12 as the proton-flux measurement, the subtractions are
`WIC -= (P1/P2) SI12` and `SI13 -= (P3/P2) SI12`. These coefficients are
8.96/0.123 at 2-keV mean proton energy but 35.35/0.424 at 25 keV. In the
published event's 17--20 MLT proton sector, 1,158 of 1,286 initially guarded
pixels survive the 2-keV correction, whereas only 373 survive at 25 keV. Frey
explicitly says energetic protons account for most WIC/SI13 signal there, so
the dark final panel largely indicates removal of proton-dominated support.
The paper does not state its Figure-16 proton-energy map, though its general
method uses the spatial Hardy model. This can explain why the present final
panel changes little, but not why Frey's uncorrected energy is higher before
any proton correction.

The cited Hubert et al. (2002) method is the closest additional description:
SI13 is mapped into 256x256 WIC image space, the ratio and mean energy are
calculated per pixel, and WIC supplies the energy flux. It does not specify how
the mapping is performed or give a smoothing/masking rule. It does, however,
calculate response curves versus viewing angle and use the curve appropriate to
the actual pixel. Its nadir-only sensitivity test changed hemispheric power by
12--17%. Frey's plotted Figure-7 relation is explicitly for nadir, so the
current static lookup misses a documented element of the cited implementation.
Coumans et al.
(2002) uses PSF smoothing and time averaging for IMAGE--NOAA validation, not
for WIC/SI13 inversion. Immel et al. (2000) supports the dayglow model, and
the Mende instrument papers support onboard TDI/distortion correction and
calibration. Meurant et al. (2003) explicitly coregisters and smooths WIC/SI13,
but Frey does not cite it; this is evidence for a related implementation, not
proof of the Figure-16 procedure.

Frey also states that its high-activity 24 June 2000 FAST validation case
(Figure 15) required a disturbed-atmosphere correction because enhanced oxygen
absorption reduced SI13 and otherwise produced unreasonably high inferred
energy. The wording points to a modified atmosphere-dependent Figure-7
response rather than an image operation, but no atmosphere profile, corrected
curve, formula, scale factor, or implementation citation is published. Frey
does not say that this correction was used for Figure 16. This is distinct from
smoothing, count masking, and temporal gain correction. The exact Figure-16
processing remains underdocumented.
