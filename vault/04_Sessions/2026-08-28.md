# Session — Frey Figure-16 preprocessing

Last reviewed: 2026-08-28

The raw-`img` reconstruction was repeated with manually selected nightside
histogram knees, most recently WIC 1000, SI12 15, and SI13 8 counts. It leaves
a strong dayside band that is absent from Frey's Figure 16. Re-reading the
papers resolves why: Frey et al. used a spatial, instrument-specific quiet-time
dayglow model depending on solar and spacecraft zenith angles, sensitivity,
and F10.7. Meurant et al. used a brightness-histogram background estimate in a
nightside-only study and explicitly required no airglow correction. The two
methods should not be treated as one interchangeable preprocessing step.

The supplied FUVVIEW3 call chain was checked directly. `getudf_var.pro` calls
the empirical dayglow wrapper, but that wrapper invokes the background routine
with `/nof107` and iteration zero, disabling its F10.7 branch. This differs
from Frey's written description; whether it also describes the exact
historical dataset-production configuration remains unverified.

## Meurant Figures 7 and 9 reconstruction

`scripts/debugging/recreate_meurant_figures7_9.py` reconstructs the two
keograms from 86 native orbit-0364 frames spanning 10:04--12:58 UT. SI12 and
SI13 are footprint-mapped into the WIC detector frame before histogram
background removal, smoothing, proton correction, and the published MLT/MLAT
averages. The WIC evolution agrees well in structure with the paper. The
reconstructed ratio is much more frequently above the paper's 130 upper scale,
especially in Figure 9, so the excessive-ratio problem remains under this
historical nightside-style treatment. Fixed knees and a 1.5-pixel Gaussian are
explicit approximations that can be varied from the command line. The current
figures use the pipeline-consistent 2-keV proton mean energy.

The white ratio regions were checked against the saved data. NaNs are plotted
black; at 2-keV proton mean energy, 3.4% of finite Figure-7 bins and 25.4% of
finite Figure-9 bins exceed 130. The code averages finite pixelwise ratios, so
very small positive corrected-SI13 denominators can produce extreme ratios and
dominate a bin. An undocumented historical validity threshold remains a
plausible missing step.

Meurant's wording supports the present mean-of-pixelwise-ratios calculation:
the ratio image is constructed first and its pixels are subsequently averaged
in each keogram bin. The WIC >= 50 and SI13 >= 3 guard is therefore proposed
only as a sensitivity test. Those values come from modern Gasparini/legacy
icBuilder low-count handling, not an explicit Meurant threshold.

A parallel 50/3-mask reconstruction was generated. At 2 keV proton mean
energy, it reduces finite bins above 130 from 3.4% to 1.0% in Figure 7 and from
25.4% to 10.3% in Figure 9. The large-ratio tail is greatly reduced but not
eliminated. The paper confirms
that SI12 is used to infer and remove proton contamination from WIC and SI13,
but does not document how the histogram background threshold was selected.

Frey's proton correction uses the same SI12-based modeled-emission method.
The response tables are indexed by proton mean energy `<E>`. The current
`icphysics.image` comments incorrectly call that axis characteristic energy;
the pipeline's 2-keV setting is a mean proton energy.

The 8-to-2-keV rerun changed the over-range fractions only modestly and did not
change the keogram morphology, so proton-energy choice is not the main ratio
problem. Frey generally used a Hardy-model proton mean energy, explicitly 25
keV in its June-2000 validation example; no value is given for Figure 16.

The supplied IDL records and FUVVIEW3 support databases contain enough
information for a Python approximation of the SI12/SI13 active dayglow model.
The raw-count requirement comes from processing order, not from the model:
FUVVIEW3 flat-fields raw counts internally to fit the quiet-time table, returns
the fitted background to raw space, subtracts it, and then calibrates the
residual. A Python diagnostic can keep the saved image and modeled background
in calibrated/flat-fielded space instead. Directly calling the old wrapper on
the saved image would incorrectly apply the flat field twice.

## Frey-event CIMAGE comparison

The separately obtained `wic20003021138.idl` is the exact orbit-0364 frame at
11:38:24 used in the Frey Figure-16 reconstruction. Its detector geometry is
identical to the NetCDF frame, and its `IMAGE` agrees closely with NetCDF
`img` (`r=0.9995`), ruling out an orientation or frame-selection ambiguity.

The clean `frey_figure16_or_0364.png` used fuvpy `shimg` for its middle WIC
panel. On the 5,955 polar pixels where either `CIMAGE` or `shimg` is at least
500 counts, `CIMAGE` and `shimg` agree extremely well (`r=0.998`). A direct
fit gives `CIMAGE = 0.991 shimg + 107 counts`; the median difference is
`+88 counts`, or about `+4.6%` relative to `shimg`. Their background fields
still differ, but the bright auroral signal relevant to the ratio is nearly
the same. This event-specific comparison therefore points away from the WIC
background product as the explanation for the excessive WIC/SI13 ratios.

The diagnostic is in `scripts/debugging/compare_frey_cimage_event.py`; its
PNG and PDF are saved under `figures/debugging/paper_reconstruction/`.

## Background-induced WIC/SI13 scale change

The 11:38:24 Frey frame was also used to compare each co-registered pixel with
itself before and after fuvpy background subtraction, before proton
correction. WIC uses `img` versus `shimg`; SI13 uses `img` versus `dgimg` and
is footprint-mapped onto WIC once using fixed paired support. This does not
estimate the absolute stellar calibration. It isolates the change imposed on
the already calibrated WIC/SI13 ratio by the background processing.

For all 15,113 positive paired pixels, the median
`R_after / R_before` is 0.519, with a 10--90% range of 0.068--0.923. Under the
familiar diagnostic guard `WIC >= 50`, `SI13 >= 3`, the median is 0.648 and
the 10--90% range is 0.203--0.887. The scale change is strongly signal
dependent and becomes unstable as corrected SI13 approaches zero. It is
therefore not a replacement uniform gain. For this frame the background
processing generally lowers, rather than raises, WIC/SI13, so it cannot
explain the excessive ratios as an upward rescaling; weak-SI13 background
residuals can nevertheless dominate individual ratios.

The diagnostic is `scripts/debugging/compare_background_ratio_scale.py`; the
PNG and PDF are under `figures/debugging/paper_reconstruction/`.

The ratio change was then decomposed exactly as
`R_after/R_before = (WIC_after/WIC_before)/(SI13_after/SI13_before)`. On the
9,016 signal pixels, fuvpy removes a median 50.8% of the pre-background WIC
signal (10--90%: 18.5--93.3%) and 18.8% of SI13 (5.1--70.6%). WIC loses the
larger fraction in 99.4% of these pixels. This does not establish that WIC is
over-subtracted, because the pre-background WIC contains substantial
background/dayglow. It does establish that this processing stage drives the
ratio downward almost everywhere; SI13 over-subtraction is not creating the
event's generally high ratio. The decomposition script and figure are
`scripts/debugging/decompose_background_ratio_scale.py` and
`frey_background_fraction_decomposition_or_0364.{png,pdf}`.

## Sensor-specific fuvpy background treatment

Code inspection corrects an important omission in the preceding diagnostics.
The fuvpy `backgroundmodel_BS` and `backgroundmodel_SH` implementations have
no separate WIC, SI12, or SI13 model branches: all three use the same basis,
geometry variables, robust iteration, and equations, while their noise and
weights are estimated independently from each input stack. Sensor-specific
handling occurs around those functions. WIC alone is reflattened by default,
WIC and SI have different bad-pixel masks, and WIC nonpositive input pixels
are discarded while SI zero counts remain valid.

More importantly, fuvpy's own dayglow-paper figure workflow supplies different
regularization: WIC uses BS/SH damping `1e-2/1e-4`, whereas SI12 and SI13 use
`1e-1/1e1`. The current icBuilder orbit builder instead applies
`1e-3/1e-4` to every sensor. Downstream Product 1 uses WIC `shimg` but SI12 and
SI13 `dgimg`, although it multiplies both DG and SH weights for every sensor.
The SI intensity could therefore have been sensitive to the mismatched BS
damping, while its binning weight is also influenced by an SI SH fit made with
WIC-like damping. The existing external NetCDFs do not record these
parameters, so their provenance is inferred from the current builder rather
than proven from file metadata. This motivated fixed-support, full-orbit tests
for SI12 and SI13 on orbit 0364.

The first sensor-specific rerun was completed for the full 247-frame SI12
orbit 0364. Relative to the stored current products, the paper-workflow
damping (`BS=1e-1`, `SH=1e1`) leaves `dgimg` effectively unchanged across
2,452,239 common pixels: `r=0.9999994`, median absolute difference
`0.00077 count`, and a 10--90% signed-difference range of
`-0.0030--+0.0021 count`. The SH solution changes more, but remains small in
intensity terms: `shimg r=0.9955`, median absolute difference `0.360 count`,
and 10--90% range `-0.673--+0.881 count`. Product 1 uses SI12 `dgimg`, so the
SI12 intensity entering proton correction is insensitive to this damping
change. The impact on SI12 `shweight`, which Product 1 currently multiplies
into its binning weight despite using `dgimg`, remains to be checked. This
negative SI12 result was followed by the more important SI13 test.

The SI13 rerun reaches the same conclusion for the ratio denominator. Across
2,634,145 common `dgimg` pixels, the current and paper-workflow results have
`r=0.9999917`, median absolute difference `0.00233 count`, and a signed
10--90% difference range of `-0.0109--+0.0182 count`. SI13 `shimg` is more
sensitive (`r=0.9718`, median absolute difference `0.553 count`, signed
10--90% range `-1.236--+1.136 count`), but Product 1 uses SI13 `dgimg` for
the intensity. The damping mismatch therefore does not explain the excessive
WIC/SI13 ratio through SI13 brightness on this event. Its possible effect
through the SH-derived binning weight remains separate. The reproducible test
is `scripts/debugging/compare_background_damping.py`; figures are
`si12_background_damping_comparison_or_0364.{png,pdf}` and
`si13_background_damping_comparison_or_0364.{png,pdf}`.

The WIC side was then tested over the same complete orbit by changing only BS
damping from `1e-3` to the paper value `1e-2`; SH damping remained `1e-4`.
The final `shimg` has `r=0.9999971`, median absolute difference `0.156 count`,
median signed difference `+0.0288 count`, and a signed 10--90% range of
`-0.509--+0.364 count` over 10.44 million pixels. Thus the WIC damping mismatch
also cannot account for the ratio scale. The comparison is
`wic_background_damping_comparison_or_0364.{png,pdf}`.

A follow-up sensitivity analysis reconstructed the final-weight BS
`diag(G.T @ G)` for ten common WIC/SI13 orbits spread from 0085 to 1930. The
median diagonal varies by `28.4x` for WIC (`3.38e3--9.58e4`) and `12.8x` for
SI13 (`2.02e3--2.57e4`). Fixed damping is therefore not scale-invariant between
orbits. However, the current damping-to-median-diagonal ratio is only
`1.0e-8--5.0e-7`; even the paper settings give only `1.0e-7--3.0e-6` for WIC
and `3.9e-6--5.0e-5` for SI13. This supports the empirical result that changing
damping barely changes the images. The diagnostic deliberately accumulates
only the diagonal and uses stored final robust weights; eigenvalue analysis
would be needed to quantify the weakest model directions. See
`scripts/debugging/analyze_background_gtg_scale.py` and
`background_gtg_scale.{csv,png,pdf}`.
