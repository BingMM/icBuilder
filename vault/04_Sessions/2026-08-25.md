# Session — Corpus-wide WIC/SI13 ratio diagnosis

Last reviewed: 2026-08-25

## Result

The high WIC/SI13 ratios are a corpus-wide problem, not an isolated Coumans
event. `scripts/debugging/audit_large_ratio_corpus.py` streamed all 1,684 local
image-ratio Product-2 orbit files (450,601 frames) without changing the
products. After proton correction, 38.34% of all positive pixel-frames exceed
the Frey-table maximum `R=136.486`. With the diagnostic guard `WIC >= 50` and
`SI13 >= 3`, 14.48% remain above it; even `SI13 >= 20` leaves 6.40%.

The largest tail is concentrated at weak SI13, but weak SI13 is not a complete
explanation. Proton correction changes the distribution only modestly. The
failure fraction has recurring seasonal structure, weak association with Kp,
and stronger association with WIC weight and SI13 coverage. These statistics
describe correlated pixel-frames and do not define independent samples or a
historical Coumans validity rule. The association calculation uses one
equal-weighted row per orbit; full-array weight summaries can proxy season,
viewing, masking, or background-fit behavior and are not causal diagnostics.

## Focused stage budget

The orbit-0968 detector-space diagnostic compared raw mapping, background
subtraction, common smoothing, and proton correction. Current fuvpy background
subtraction reduces the three-frame median ratio from 233 to 93; smoothing and
2-keV proton correction change it only slightly. Background subtraction also
changes the finite positive support, so the 233-to-93 change is not a
fixed-pixel estimate. Halving the SI12 proton contribution and estimating
histogram backgrounds after mapping SI to WIC do not repair the result.
Constant-field mapping is exact, subtraction commutes with the linear mapping
to numerical precision, and the camera morphology shows no row/column
reversal.

The tests therefore rule out a simple integration-time conversion, ratio
orientation, proton-correction magnitude, smoothing, scalar histogram
background, or numerical mapping bug. They do not validate physical camera
pointing/coregistration.

## fuvpy and upstream provenance

The local fuvpy implementation copies the IDL `IMAGE` field and selected
geometry, applies an additional WIC-only reflat, and performs no temporal
gain, temperature, voltage, or exposure correction itself. It drops upstream
calibration flags, detector voltages, source identifiers, WIC `CIMAGE`, and
other housekeeping information. Published descriptions say FUVVIEW3 products
nominally contain calibrated counts, but the exact recipe/version used for
these local files and its compatibility with the Frey response have not been
verified.

The `reflat` option cannot supply a missing SI flat field: fuvpy invokes its
reflat routine only for WIC. Frey et al. describe SI flat-field lookup tables
and temporal sensitivity corrections as required preprocessing, so those
corrections must already be included in the upstream `IMAGE` field for the
Frey response tables to apply.

## Next action

Resolve the upstream FUVVIEW3 calibration lineage with the dataset producer or
original software, concentrating on the SI13 temporal gain/flat-field version
and calibration-flag meaning. Do not alter Product 2 on the current evidence.
Until the lineage is established, ratios outside the Frey response domain
should be treated as retrieval failures rather than evidence of very high
electron energy.
