
## Model Resolution Analysis: 3D Spline Basis

### 1. Model Parameters
* **Spatial Basis:** Cubic B-Spline ($p=3$), Clamped.
    * Domain: $L = 8095 \text{ km}$
    * Control Points: $N = 30$
* **Temporal Basis:** Quadratic B-Spline ($p=2$), clamped.
    * Knot Spacing: $\Delta t = 5 \text{ min}$
    * Data Sampling: $2 \text{ min}$

### 2. Spatial Resolution
For a clamped B-spline, the number of non-zero knot spans is defined as $N - p$.

* **Grid Resolution (Knot Spacing):**
    $$\Delta x_{grid} = \frac{L}{N - p} = \frac{8095 \text{ km}}{27} \approx \mathbf{300 \text{ km}}$$
    *This represents the degrees of freedom in the model.*

* **Effective Feature Resolution:**
    $$\lambda_{min} \approx 2 \times \Delta x_{grid} \approx \mathbf{600 \text{ km}}$$
    *Based on the Nyquist-Shannon limit, roughly two control points are required to define a distinct spatial feature (peak + trough).*

### 3. Temporal Resolution
While the data is sampled every 2 minutes, the resolution is limited by the stiffness of the spline basis function.

* **Grid Resolution (Knot Spacing):** $\mathbf{5 \text{ min}}$
    * The model can mathematically represent a change in gradient every 5 minutes.
* **Effective Feature Resolution:** $\mathbf{10 \text{ min}}$
    * **Periodic Signals:** A full wave cycle (up-down-return) requires a minimum of two intervals ($2 \times 5 \text{ min}$) to be resolved without aliasing.
    * **Impulse Response:** Due to the support width of a quadratic spline ($(p+1)\Delta t = 15 \text{ min}$), a single instantaneous "spike" in the real physical system will be smeared into a 15-minute response in the model.

> **Note on Resolution vs. Scale Size:**
> While the model contains a knot every 5 minutes, we cannot claim a 5-minute resolution.
>
> * **The Aliasing Trap:** A feature with a scale size of 5 minutes (e.g., a peak at $t=2.5$ and return to zero at $t=5$) creates a "blind spot." If the feature falls purely between knots, the spline basis cannot capture it, potentially resulting in a null solution.
> * **Conclusion:** We guarantee the resolution of **10-minute structures** (wavelengths), as these are sufficiently large to be captured regardless of their phase alignment with the knot grid.

### 4. Edge Effects (Padding Requirement)
Due to the clamped boundary condition and the stiffness of the spline, the first and last intervals are subject to high-amplitude "ringing" if the data gradient is steep.
* **Mitigation:** The temporal domain should include **10–15 minutes of padding** pre- and post-event during inversion to absorb these boundary oscillations, which are then discarded in the final analysis.
* Currently padding is not implemented and the 0th Order Tikhonov regularization is left addressing it.

### 5. Impact of Regularization (0th Order Tikhonov)
The model utilizes **0th Order Tikhonov Regularization** (Damping), which minimizes the norm of the model coefficients ($||\mathbf{m}||_2^2$).

* **Resolution Preservation:** Unlike 1st or 2nd order regularization (which penalize roughness and act as a low-pass filter/blur), 0th order regularization does **not** degrade the effective spatial or temporal resolution.
* **Mechanism:** It penalizes the *amplitude* of the control points, not the *gradient* between them.
    * **Benefit:** It stabilizes the inverse problem by suppressing "exploding" coefficients in areas of poor data coverage.
    * **Result:** The "sharpness" of the basis functions is preserved. While the *magnitude* of a recovered feature might be slightly damped (biased toward zero), its *width* (temporal duration or spatial extent) remains dictated purely by the spline basis and knot spacing.
